Recruiting Agent Proposal

October 15, 2026 · Michele Burns

One recruiting agent, built in stages, to cut manual work in US and EU hiring and give leadership data it can trust.

Demos below use sample data. Nothing here is connected to any company systems.

The proposal at a glance

We want one recruiting agent that US and EU recruiters can type into, backed by Workday and LinkedIn data, so routine work happens automatically and every number leaders see is consistent.

Today recruiters re-enter the same data in Excel, SharePoint and Workday, chase candidates by hand, and rebuild comp benchmarks from scratch. That costs time, invites errors, and leaves candidates waiting. The five pieces below use Microsoft tools most companies already have, and none needs a Workday change to start.

1. Comp benchmark agent

Anyone on the team types a role and location, and the agent returns a benchmark chart and one-page summary using the approved rules.

Why it is a good idea: Benchmarks are rebuilt by hand each time, and our market data is weak. A shared agent applies the same method and data every time and saves each result, so the next request starts from what already exists.

What the result shows: base pay and bonus broken out, the total, the years of experience it was based on, and where on the web each figure came from. It also shows the Workday salary range and midpoint, because offers in this example usually stay at or below midpoint. If requirements are loosened, such as location, industry or certifications, it shows how much the pay goes down.

What it needs: market data for the role and country, access to the Workday pay range (ranges, not individual salaries), and written rules from Total Rewards.

2. EU intake, enter once

One short form captures a candidate's details a single time and creates everything else from it. Today the same details are typed into Excel, a SharePoint folder and Workday. With the form, the tracker row and folder are created automatically, and the Workday steps arrive as a checklist.

Why it is a good idea: Re-entry costs time and causes mismatches between systems. The form can also build in EU rules, such as consent and retention dates, so they are not left to memory.

Tracking each candidate through hiring: The tracker row also carries the Req #, hiring manager, the date the EOR contract was sent, the start date, the background check status and the offer details (base comp, bonus, RSUs and sign-on), so one row shows where every EU candidate stands.

Third-party vendors: Roles that run through an outside vendor add a vendor branch to the form, with vendor name, req ID and a restricted submission folder.

Longer term: Ask HRIS whether the missing EU fields can live in Workday itself. If so, Workday becomes the only place to enter data and the Excel tracker goes away.

3. Automatic follow-ups and reply handling

The agent drafts a follow-up after 7 days of silence, then stops the moment a candidate replies. A person approves drafts at first, and sending can become automatic once the templates are trusted. A "not interested" reply tags the candidate, stops further emails and prepares a note for Workday.

Why it is a good idea: Follow-ups are routine and easy to forget, and candidates who hear nothing form a poor view of the company. This gives every candidate a consistent response and removes manual tracking.

Limits to know about: Writing the note straight into Workday needs an integration built by HRIS or IT. Until then the agent prepares the note for a person to paste in. EU candidates need a consent check before anything is sent automatically.

4. Recruiting dashboard (long-term)

Workday and LinkedIn statistics in one place, with each candidate counted once and LinkedIn shown as the source.

Why it is a good idea: Stats are pulled by hand from two systems today, and applicants who come through LinkedIn can be counted twice. One dashboard gives leadership numbers they can trust, for US and EU roles together.

Why it is long-term: It depends on turning on LinkedIn Recruiter System Connect, getting a Workday report from HRIS, and agreeing metric definitions. A basic Excel version can come first while those are in progress. It is also the base for a live Workday connection to the agent later.

5. Interview scheduling in one step

Workday should schedule panel interviews directly into Outlook, with the Teams link and the candidate's email already on the invite. Today I schedule in Workday, then open Outlook, add a Teams link, and add the candidate's email by hand. With the integration working, one step in Workday creates the invite for the panel and the candidate, and a single calendar view shows when the panel is free.

What Workday supports: Workday has a setting to turn on Microsoft Outlook interview scheduling, which lets recruiters see interviewer availability and responses, and an option to add a web conference link once a web conference integration is set up. See Workday's interview scheduling documentation. These are settings HRIS enables, so the first step is finding out what is already on at the company.

To confirm: whether the candidate receives the calendar invite directly or an email from Workday, and whether the Teams link needs its own web conference setup.

Easier calendar view: The agent can also answer "When are these four interviewers free next week?" from Outlook availability, so panels are not built by checking calendars one at a time. IT would need to approve that access.

Recruiters get hours back, the numbers become reliable, and candidates hear from us on time. My early estimate is 5 to 10 hours a week back per recruiter, which we should confirm by tracking time on these tasks for two weeks before launch.
